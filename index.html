Between 1784 and 1894 the United States took more than 1.5 billion acres from Indigenous nations.

Here are the 595 treaties and agreements signed between 1784 and 1911, with the text of each, the land it took, the payment it promised, and the acts of Congress and executive orders that later overrode it.

Cession areas: Royce (1899), U.S. Forest Service digitization · Treaties and texts: Kappler; Deloria and DeMallie; Spirling (2012) · Scans: National Archives

Treaties

The cession areas are the 718 tracts Charles C. Royce mapped in Indian Land Cessions in the United States (Eighteenth Annual Report of the Bureau of American Ethnology, 1899), as digitized by the U.S. Forest Service with Claudio Saunt’s links to Royce’s schedule. Royce’s areas overlap, because the same ground was often reserved by one treaty and ceded by a later one. Each area is colored by the earliest treaty, statute or order Royce records for it, and acreage totals count each piece of ground once, under its earliest date.

The 595 treaties and agreements, 1784 to 1911, follow the list Arthur Spirling (2012) compiled from Charles Kappler’s Indian Affairs: Laws and Treaties and from Vine Deloria Jr. and Raymond DeMallie’s Documents of American Indian Diplomacy. Scans are from the National Archives’ Ratified Indian Treaties series, by way of the Indigenous Digital Archive’s Treaties Explorer.

Payment is the money a treaty promises: one-time sums and goods at their stated value, and annuities over their stated terms. Annuities with no end date, payments by the head, and goods or services given no value are reported beside the total and not added to it. Payment totals are given for the 260 treaties with mapped land. Figures on land-grant universities are from High Country News. Land taken without a federal treaty lies outside Royce’s maps and is dated by independence, state admission, transfer or the end of Indigenous possession. Present-day shares use the Census Bureau’s 2023 reservation and trust land boundaries and the Forest Service’s administrative forest boundaries. Royce’s schedule ends in 1894; allotment and later takings are described but not mapped. Alaska and Hawaiʻi are not covered.
